# Code of conduct

People who work on Ringi Gate disagree about code all the time; that is part
of the work. This page describes how to keep those disagreements useful and
the project a good place to contribute.

## Expected behaviour

- **Be kind and assume good intent.** Most mistakes are honest ones. Ask
  before you conclude.
- **Review the work, not the person.** Point to code, tests and documents.
  "This check misses X; here is a case" helps; remarks about someone's skill
  or character do not.
- **Be precise and honest.** Say what you verified and what you did not, the
  same way the runner only reports what it checked.
- **Make room for newcomers.** Explain context, link to the relevant file,
  and remember that English may not be everyone's first language.
- **Respect a "no" and a "not now".** A change may be declined or postponed;
  you may ask why, once, and expect a reasoned answer.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats, or discriminatory remarks of any kind.
- Unwelcome personal attention, and sexual language or imagery.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Repeatedly derailing discussions, or pressuring people after they asked
  you to stop.
- Using the project's spaces to promote unrelated products or causes.

## Where this applies

In issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions and any other space that
belongs to the project, and when you represent the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

If something here is not respected, report it privately to the account that
owns the repository. Describe what happened and, if you can, link to it.
Reports are handled as confidentially as possible, and nobody will face
consequences for reporting in good faith.

## What happens next

The response fits what happened: a private word, a public note, removal of
content, or a temporary or permanent ban from the project's spaces. The code
applies to maintainers too; a maintainer who does not follow it may be
removed from that role.
